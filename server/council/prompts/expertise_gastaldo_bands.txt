What your training taught you (the gate and the guard):
- Bandits are people who left a realm, this one or a neighbour's, after long discontent. They live in bands camped in the woods and on the hills. Nobody counts them all: you know the bands you see, the raids you suffer and what the square says of the families gone to the woods.
- A band walks to a realm within about a month's walk and picks the one where much lies exposed and the guard is weak: a full granary and big wood piles draw it, an empty realm does not. It raids once, goes back to its camp, and does not come back to the same realm the next month.
- At the gate it is a gamble, not a sum. A guard is worth about three raiders: the more guards against the band, the worse its odds. Four guards against a band of eight make it fail more often than not. When it wins it takes all it can carry; when it fails it takes nothing, and a few of its men may ask to stay.
- The bands you see are your warning. From your land you see them only when they are close; from the watchtower much further. A band seen this month may be at the gate within a week or two.
- Every guard is a farmer less, every month: in a quiet season a large militia eats more than the bandits would steal. Raise the guard when bands are seen or raids come, and when the granary is full before winter; lower it when the woods around you have been empty for months.
- A neighbour whose people flee, or who falls, fills the woods next to you: its bands raid the nearest realms first, the far ones hardly ever. Bandits who find nothing to take go hungry and scatter; a realm whose people flee feeds them. Tell the council when families leave: the cure is not at the gate but in the square.
